FIG 1 Drawing a stroke

37 calls · 6 levels deep

Three entry points, one gesture. The thing worth noticing is that the pen builds its arc chain while you draw — addSample rebuilds only the two gaps a new sample disturbs — so what is on screen under the pen is already the geometry the resolve will use, and pen-up does not change the shape.

FIG 2 Resolving strokes, and folding in erasers

35 calls · 5 levels deep

Nothing here runs on a frame. It is a timer that re-enters until its queues drain, standing aside while the camera moves. Outlines resolve before erasers are folded in, because an eraser cannot be subtracted until it has one — and neither can the ink beneath it.

FIG 3 Erasing: the cut

43 calls · 7 levels deep

One decision at the top governs everything below it: is the target at or below the level the erase was made at, or above it? At or below, the cut happens here. Above, the work descends instead (Fig 4). The cut is one job, cutJob: the same function in the erase worker and inline, with the document side applied on the main thread by _applyCut.

FIG 4 Erasing below an object's own level

26 calls · 4 levels deep

One job for the whole family. Every cede is virtual, each level's square ink is derived exactly as the tile store derives it, and nothing touches the document until the whole descent has succeeded. Every step hands ink down; only the last one cuts. Skipping the chain and jumping straight to the erase level makes the ceded tile exactly zero units wide at five crossings.

FIG 5 Rendering a frame

92 calls · 10 levels deep

The widest tree here, and the one with a cycle in it: _ensureUp reaches its parent's tiles one level at a time. A composed long jump upward cancels catastrophically, which is why the chain exists at all. Both renderers draw the same render list; the raster one is the default, the SVG one is behind ?svg.

FIG 6 Zooming and panning

41 calls · 9 levels deep

Every camera gesture funnels into _settle, which crosses and shifts until the camera is in a legal state. The needs* questions afterwards decide the only thing that matters for cost: a new render list, or the same one moved.

FIG 7 Selecting and moving

49 calls · 6 levels deep

A press changes nothing: a tap selects at pen-up, and a drag starts past SELECT_DRAG_PX. A drag never touches a stored coordinate: whole cells go into the address, the rest into the object's displacement table, and a selected frame moves as a frame.
